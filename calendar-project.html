Reducing Group Scheduling Friction

HangoutCalendar is a cross-platform scheduling app designed to reduce the time and effort students spend coordinating group meetings.

It combines shared group availability with a synced personal calendar to make finding and planning meeting times faster and more organized.

Context

A seven-week academic project completed from October to December 2024 for an app development class.

Contributions

I primarily focused on product design, with additional contributions to front-end development.

Problem Space

Group scheduling often involves too much back-and-forth.

Group scheduling can require users to compare separate calendars, reminders, and messages to find times that work.

Existing tools aren’t really designed to address this: Google Calendar and Outlook focus mainly on individual scheduling, while tools such as Calendly are better suited for one-off appointments.

Opportunity

Reduce the effort required to coordinate group meetings.

We saw an opportunity to bring group availability and planning into one place instead of relying on scattered tools and conversations.

Research + Insights

Understanding where coordination breaks down.

We conducted informal interviews with underclassmen and gathered feedback on early prototypes to understand how they currently coordinate meetings.

Our main takeaways were:

Users want control over what they share with different groups.

Participants wanted to keep some calendar details private depending on the group. What they shared with friends, classmates, and family was not always the same.

Users rely on workarounds to share their availability.

Participants often had to send screenshots, messages, or manually explain their availability because their calendars were not designed to be easily shared in a group context.

In larger groups, delayed responses could also make it harder to maintain a shared understanding of availability.

Design Focus

This led us to ask:

How might we make shared availability easy to compare and confirm while giving users control over what they share?

Iterations

Organizing scheduling into dedicated group spaces.

We organized planning around group spaces where members could view that specific group’s availability and confirmed events in one place

Early versions displayed scheduled events and all unavailable times together in one shared timetable. Testing showed that combining both created too much visual clutter which made the calendar harder to scan.

Thus, we separated the two needs: the group main page focused on confirmed events, while availability appeared only when users were creating a new event.

Making week-to-month navigation feel seamless.

We also explored how users could move between week and month views intuitively without relying on separate controls common in existing calendar interfaces.

Horizontal swipes move forward and backward through time, while vertical gestures adjust the level of detail between week and month views. Keeping each direction tied to a consistent action helped make the transition between views feel more predictable and continuous.

Solution Briefing

HangoutCalendar

The final design centers around two connected systems: dedicated group scheduling spaces and a synced personal calendar

Familiar interaction patterns reduce onboarding friction.

HangoutCalendar uses familiar patterns from messaging and social apps, including profiles, contacts, groups, and bottom navigation.

This gives users a recognizable structure for moving between personal calendars, group calendars, and account settings.

Group spaces keep plans and availability in context.

Each group acts as its own scheduling space where users can view members, see group events and suggest new events.

When creating events, unavailable blocks are shown in a shared timetable based on each member's personal calendar to keep each member’s privacy.

A synced personal calendar keeps commitments connected.

The personal calendar brings group plans and personal commitments into one place.

Confirmed group events sync automatically, while external calendars such as Google Calendar can also be imported to keep the schedule up to date.

Reflection

Prioritizing the core experience kept the project achievable.

With a seven-week timeline, we separated features essential to demonstrating HangoutCalendar's core value from features that could be deferred.

We prioritized reliable group scheduling, shared availability, calendar syncing, and core navigation over secondary features such as dedicated notifications, colour coded calendars, and a more complex hour-by-hour day view.

This helped us deliver a cohesive working app within the timeframe and reinforced the importance of prioritizing the core user experience over feature completeness.
