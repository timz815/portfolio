Sustaining Orchestra Audience Engagement

A proposed communication system designed to help a community orchestra build attendance beyond its peak holiday concert.

By making future concerts easier to discover and plan for, the system can help increase audience engagement across the season without creating significant new work for the orchestra.

Context

An academic project completed from September to December 2025 for a UX research and design class.

The project is not affiliated with White Rock City Orchestra.

Contributions

I focused on the design strategy through problem framing and research, and led the communication strategy to structure a clear and persuasive design rationale.

Problem Space

Attendance drops outside WRCO's peak holiday concert.

White Rock City Orchestra attracts large audiences for its Christmas concert, while attendance during the spring and summer can fluctuate.

This imbalance can make planning and programming across the rest of the season more difficult.

Client Goals

Increase off-season attendance without increasing operational burden.

WRCO wanted to reduce this uneven attendance while working within the limited capacity of a small community organization.

Any proposed solution therefore needed to be simple to implement and easy to maintain.

Opportunity

Use the Christmas concert to build momentum for the rest of the season.

The holiday concert already brings a large audience into contact with WRCO as they look for festive outings.

We saw an opportunity to use that moment to make upcoming concerts more visible and encourage audiences to stay connected beyond December.

Research

We looked at what influences people to commit to a concert.

We ran two co-creation workshops with seven concert-goers and developed user personas to explore what shapes attendance decisions and the broader concert experience.

The research surfaced three patterns that shaped our direction:

One person often plans for the whole group.

Many attendance decisions were coordinated by a single organizer.

They depended on clear dates and ticket purchasing times to make plans with others. When those details were harder to find, committing to the event became harder.

The experience starts before the music does.

Participants' memories of an event were also shaped by moments before the show and during intermission.

Lightweight, optional engagement during these moments can dramatically increase the perceived value of the experience itself.

Future concerts disappear from the experience too easily.

Mapping the audience journey showed that communication was concentrated around the current concert with few opportunities to keep upcoming events visible before attendees left the experience.

Design Focus

This led us to ask:

How might WRCO maintain momentum across the season by improving communication and making better use of existing audience touchpoints?

Iterations

Improving individual touchpoints wasn't enough.

Our first concepts explored improving separate parts of the concert journey including the post-concert experience, the sponsorship page, and the website landing page.

Each concept addressed a specific moment but each standalone improvement was not impactful enough.

This shifted our thinking to begin looking at how WRCO's existing touchpoints could work together and multiply each other’s impact across the season.

Solution Briefing

A Revamped Communication Package

We proposed a communication system built around channels WRCO already uses including its website, email, printed concert booklet, and in-person concert experience.

Each touchpoint reinforces upcoming concerts at a different stage of the audience journey without requiring WRCO to maintain a new platform or take on significant operational overhead.

Solution

The booklet turns the current concert into a touchpoint for the next one.

The booklet makes use of natural downtime before the performance and during intermission to surface upcoming concerts and relevant offers.

As a take-home artifact, it also supports planning after the event, especially for attendees who did not purchase the tickets themselves.

The website gives planners the information they need earlier.

The website brings dates, pricing, and ticket options forward so organizers can evaluate an event without searching across the site.

It also simplifies group purchases and surfaces practical details such as parking and entry information so concert-goers know what to expect after buying their tickets.

The email keeps WRCO visible between concerts.

Rather than treating emails as standalone reminders, the sequence uses teasers and follow-ups to build anticipation around upcoming concerts.

This keeps audiences engaged between performances while giving upcoming events more consistent visibility.

In-Concert Enhancements

Take advantage of the post-show high.

Ticket giveaways, discount draws, or early-bird sales for an upcoming concert could give attendees another reason to return while enthusiasm from the current performance is still high.

Audience Journey

Each touchpoint reinforces the next chance to attend.

Together, the website, email, booklet, and in-concert experience create repeated opportunities for audiences to encounter upcoming concerts.

Reflection

Adapting our research approach kept the project moving.

As the semester progressed, WRCO shifted its attention toward preparing for a major upcoming concert, which reduced opportunities for continued collaboration and feedback.

We adapted by using research methods that placed fewer demands on the organization, including design ethnography and co-creation workshops with concert-goers as proxies for attendees.

Weekly presentations pushed me to make complex work easier to understand.

It honed my ability to communicate evolving research and design decisions more clearly and strengthened how I structure design rationale so the relationship between evidence, decisions, and the final proposal remained easy to follow.
